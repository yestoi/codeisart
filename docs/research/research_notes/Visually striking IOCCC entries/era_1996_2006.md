# Visually striking IOCCC winners, 1996 to 2006

Scope note: the IOCCC held no contest in 1997, 1999, 2002 or 2003, so this era has seven contests: 1996, 1998, 2000, 2001, 2004, 2005 and 2006. I catalogued all 97 winning entries from these years using each entry's `.entry.json` (award, author, abstract) in the official winner repo (sparse clone of github.com/ioccc-src/winner, branch `master`, commit `31bd35c`, dated 2026-09-28). The full award list per year is at https://www.ioccc.org/years.html.

Method for the "tested" claims below. I built the candidates on aarch64 Linux in Docker on Apple Silicon: the same ARM 64-bit instruction set as the Pi 4, but not a Pi. I used two images: `gcc:12` (GCC 12.5.0, close to Raspberry Pi OS Bookworm's GCC 12) and `gcc:14` (GCC 14.4.0, the generation where implicit int and implicit function declarations became hard errors). I ran each program inside an 80x23 pseudo-terminal (`script` plus `stty cols 80 rows 23`, `TERM=vt100`, stdin `/dev/null`) and rendered the final screen with the Python `pyte` terminal emulator. These results are cited as **[local test]**.

## Which 1996-2006 winners are most famous for visual output or source shape?

### Takeaway
The strongest live pieces for the wall are **2006/sloane** (spinning shaded donut, 80x23 exactly), **1996/eldby** ("Best output": flying 3D ASCII spheres), **2000/thadgavin** (full-screen plasma in curses mode) and **1998/chaos** (self-rotating 3D wireframe). The strongest source-as-art pieces are **2000/dhyang** (a portrait of Saitou Hajime that prints kanji-shaped programs), **2001/herrmann2** (the source is itself a magic-eye stereogram), **1998/banks** (an aircraft-shaped flight simulator, but X11 only) and **1996/gandalf** (a figure drawn in the source). Only sloane, thadgavin, herrmann2 and dhyang score high on both axes.

### Cited Findings: per-candidate details

**1. 2006/sloane, "Homer's favorite", Andy Sloane**: https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html
- The author says "it's a little animation of a shaded donut rotating over an infinite checkerboard", with a scrolling "IOCCC 2006" ASCII logo, and that "the entire source code is shaped into a work of art, without being prefixed by any `#define` hacks, or even `#includes`" — [ioccc.org 2006/sloane](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html)
- The author also says "The shape of the source gives a hint about its output", and that it needs "some kind of VT100-ish emulation", using only the clear-screen and cursor-home escape codes — [ioccc.org 2006/sloane](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html)
- The judges said: "Looks almost good enough to eat, dunnit?" — [ioccc.org 2006/sloane](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html)
- The repo recommends `make alt`, a version with `usleep()` defaulting to 75000 µs, which it adds "to not flash too quickly, which can be problematic for some people" — [ioccc.org 2006/sloane](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html)
- The source is 33 lines, at most 56 columns wide, laid out as large block shapes. It holds a `char b[1920]` buffer (80x24) and prints 1840 characters (80x23) per frame. **[local test]**
- Measured: about 63 frames in 5 s at the default sleep (roughly 12-13 fps). Each frame is 23 newline-terminated lines of 79 characters. **[local test]**
- Builds with the repo Makefile on GCC 12 and GCC 14. A plain `gcc -std=gnu17 -Wall` fails on GCC 14 with 19 implicit-int errors; `-std=gnu89 -Wall` builds cleanly. **[local test]**
- Caveat: the famous donut-shaped `donut.c` is a different program. Sloane posted it on his blog in September 2006 and called it "my first attempt at obfuscated C" — [a1k0n.net, 2006](https://www.a1k0n.net/2006/09/15/obfuscated-c-donut.html); [a1k0n.net donut math, 2011](https://www.a1k0n.net/2011/07/20/donut-math.html). The IOCCC winner's source is block/logo shaped, not donut shaped.

**2. 2000/thadgavin, "Most portable output", Gavin Buttimore and Thaddaeus Frogley**: https://www.ioccc.org/2000/thadgavin/index.html
- The abstract reads "Prints a dazzeling moving image under multiple interfaces" (DOS VGA, SDL, or curses). The judges said it "can use curses, if the SDL graphic library is not present, and is quite good at that" — [ioccc.org 2000/thadgavin](https://www.ioccc.org/2000/thadgavin/index.html)
- The repo recommends the alt version, which has configurable `SLEEP` (curses) and `SDL_SLEEP` values and quits on `q`. The original does not call `endwin()` — [ioccc.org 2000/thadgavin](https://www.ioccc.org/2000/thadgavin/index.html)
- The source is 94 lines, at most 81 columns wide, and shaped into swirling blob or figure outlines, with the DJGPP, SDL and curses back ends each forming its own shape. **[local test]** (visual inspection of `thadgavin.c`)
- The curses back end reads `COLS` and `LINES` and uses no colour calls. In the 80x23 pty it filled the whole screen with a shaded tunnel/plasma pattern drawn in `#@=+:,.` characters. **[local test]**
- `make alt` returned an error without SDL1 installed, but the curses binary `thadgavin.alt` was still produced and ran. On the Pi, build the curses target only. A plain GCC 14 compile fails on implicit int; `-std=gnu89 -Wall` works. **[local test]**
- It redraws the full screen quickly. Use the alt build with a slower `SLEEP` for the flash governor. **[local test]**

**3. 1996/eldby, "Best output", Thor Eldby**: https://www.ioccc.org/1996/eldby/index.html
- The abstract reads "Flying 3D spheres in an ASCII display". The judges were "impressed by the author's ability to render spheres in 3D in a very small chunk of code". The author says it needs a VT100 "with 80 columns and 24 rows or more" — [ioccc.org 1996/eldby](https://www.ioccc.org/1996/eldby/index.html)
- The repo's alt version adds `usleep(35000)` "to not flash too quickly"; the original has no delay — [ioccc.org 1996/eldby](https://www.ioccc.org/1996/eldby/index.html)
- The source is a dense 9-line block of code, at most 79 columns: striking as a "wall of code", but not a picture. **[local test]**
- The frame begins with an embedded cursor-home plus the credit "Thor AAge Eldby [eldby@online.no]", then draws shaded spheres with the characters `.oegl4OS`. Measured about 26 fps (106 frames in 4 s). Each frame is 23 newline-terminated lines. **[local test]**
- Builds on GCC 12 and GCC 14 via the repo Makefile. A plain GCC 14 `-std=gnu17` build fails on implicit int; `gnu89` works. **[local test]**

**4. 2001/herrmann2, "Most eye-crossing", Immanuel Herrmann**: https://www.ioccc.org/2001/herrmann2/index.html
- The abstract reads "SIRDS/3D generator where the source is SIRDS/3D itself". The author writes: "you can create your own 3D pictures in ASCII art. And yes, the program itself is such a 3D picture." — [ioccc.org 2001/herrmann2](https://www.ioccc.org/2001/herrmann2/index.html)
- The judges wrote: "Be careful when staring at the source code - your eyes might cross" — [ioccc.org 2001/herrmann2](https://www.ioccc.org/2001/herrmann2/index.html)
- Input is a depth map on stdin (`./herrmann2 < herrmann2.ioccc`; `herrmann2.cup` is included too). This works with a canned file — [ioccc.org 2001/herrmann2](https://www.ioccc.org/2001/herrmann2/index.html)
- Output tested: a 79x24 block of random printable characters that hides a 3D image. It is static, and one line taller than the 23-row wall. **[local test]**
- Needs the repo Makefile; a plain `gcc -Wall` fails even with `-std=gnu89` because `time` and `rand` are undeclared. The Makefile build passes on GCC 12 and GCC 14. **[local test]**

**5. 2000/dhyang, "Best layout", Don Yang**: https://www.ioccc.org/2000/dhyang/index.html
- The judges called it "the single most impressive layout we've ever seen. Many programs have *one* interesting layout; this one has four." — [ioccc.org 2000/dhyang](https://www.ioccc.org/2000/dhyang/index.html)
- The author writes that "dhyang.c is an image of Saitou Hajime, which eventually generates three sources files that are images of 'aku soku zan'", a chain of mutually reproducing programs — [ioccc.org 2000/dhyang](https://www.ioccc.org/2000/dhyang/index.html)
- Output tested: the first generation is 31 lines by 78 columns, so on a 23-row wall the top 8 lines scroll away. The repo Makefile also builds the chain (`saitou`, `soku`, `zan`). **[local test]**
- Builds on GCC 12 and GCC 14 via the Makefile (5 warnings); a plain GCC 14 `gnu17` build fails, `gnu89` works. **[local test]**

**6. 1998/chaos, "Best object orientation", Alan De Smet**: https://www.ioccc.org/1998/chaos/index.html
- The author calls it "a simple ncurses 3D object viewer" and says "Given about 3 seconds of no input, it will start rotating until interrupted by user input" — [ioccc.org 1998/chaos](https://www.ioccc.org/1998/chaos/index.html)
- Data files ship with it: `cube`, `desk`, `ioccc`, `pyramid` and `xwing` (`.data`) — [repo 1998/chaos](https://github.com/ioccc-src/winner/tree/master/1998/chaos)
- Tested with no input: after the pause it rotated a wireframe drawn in `` .`: `` characters, centred in the 80x23 screen. **[local test]**
- Builds with zero warnings under both GCC 12 and GCC 14 with plain `-Wall`. **[local test]**
- The source is ordinary code with very long lines (up to 238 columns), so it is not a portrait candidate. **[local test]**

**7. 2004/newbern, "Best font engine", Jeff Newbern**: https://www.ioccc.org/2004/newbern/index.html
- The judges said: "The program is formatted using the same font glyphs as the example data file." It is a configurable `banner(1)`-like program — [ioccc.org 2004/newbern](https://www.ioccc.org/2004/newbern/index.html)
- Tested with `./newbern newbern.dat 'NOT AI'`: 156 lines of huge sideways banner letters (up to 74 columns), which scroll up the wall like a banner printout. **[local test]**

**8. 1996/westley, "Best one liner", Brian Westley**: https://www.ioccc.org/1996/westley/index.html
- The author describes a "1-line program [that] allows various analog ASCII clocks to print out the time". Three scripts draw "a grandfather clock, a mantle clock that runs backwards, and a linear-style clock" — [ioccc.org 1996/westley](https://www.ioccc.org/1996/westley/index.html)
- Tested: the grandfather clock is 22 lines by 14 columns and static. The source is a single 59-character line; the clock art lives in the shell-script arguments. **[local test]**

**9. 2006/sykes2, "Best one liner", Stephen Sykes**: https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sykes2/index.html
- The author says "The clock numerals are rendered as an old fashioned 7 segment display". Its `try.sh` recompiles and reruns the program every second, because the time comes from `__TIME__` — [ioccc.org 2006/sykes2](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sykes2/index.html)
- Tested: 7 lines by 63 columns of `!!` segments. **[local test]**

**10. 1996/gandalf, "Best layout", Dean Swift**: https://www.ioccc.org/1996/gandalf/index.html

### Inferences
- For the five-portrait set, the best pieces from this era are 2006/sloane and 2000/thadgavin (strong on both axes), 2000/dhyang or 2001/herrmann2 (famous source art with an acceptable run), and 1996/eldby or 1998/chaos (an output-led piece paired with a less pictorial source).
- 2004/newbern (prints "NOT AI" as a giant banner) and 2006/sykes2 (a clock showing its own compile time) fit the installation's theme unusually well and are cheap to run.
- 1998/banks is probably this era's most iconic shaped source, but it only works as a portrait; there is no path to the wall.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published "most famous" ranking of IOCCC entries, so fame here rests on award names and judges' remarks, not popularity data. No Hacker News or YouTube sources were consulted.
- I read source shapes in a text terminal only. The exact pictures (for example, whether sloane's blocks spell "IOCCC") should be checked by eye in a proportional preview before printing.

## Which produce terminal-only output that fits 80x23?

### Takeaway
Full-screen animations that fit: sloane, thadgavin, eldby and chaos. sloane and eldby are built for an 80x24 VT100 and print 23 newline-terminated rows, so the wall may scroll by one line per frame; thadgavin adapts to `COLS`/`LINES`. Static outputs that fit without trouble: westley (1996), sykes2, natori, toledo1 and gandalf. Outputs that are too tall and will scroll: dhyang (31 lines), hoyle (33), herrmann2 (24), newbern and ollinger (long scrolling output). None of the shortlisted terminal entries depends on ANSI colour.

### Cited Findings
- Frame geometry, from each capture split on `ESC[H`:
  - sloane: 23 newlines and 1863 bytes per frame.
  - eldby: 23 newlines and 1965 bytes per frame.
  - With 23 newlines starting at row 1 of a 23-row screen, the final newline scrolls the screen by one line before the next cursor-home.

  **[local test]**
- eldby's author requires "80 columns and 24 rows or more"; sloane's author requires "VT100-ish emulation" — [ioccc.org 1996/eldby](https://www.ioccc.org/1996/eldby/index.html); [ioccc.org 2006/sloane](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html)
- thadgavin's curses code reads `COLS` and `LINES` and calls no colour functions (no `start_color`/`init_pair`) — [repo 2000/thadgavin source](https://github.com/ioccc-src/winner/tree/master/2000/thadgavin); **[local test]** (grep of `thadgavin.alt.c`)
- Measured output sizes in the 80x23 pty, all **[local test]**:

- Frame rates measured with the repo alt defaults: sloane about 12-13 fps (`SLEEP=75000`) and eldby about 26 fps (`SLEEP=35000`). Both are full-screen redraws. **[local test]**
- The repo itself flags flashing for these: sloane's original version is described with "if you are easily overstimulated with fast movement or have photosensitivity please be careful", and thadgavin's original with "if you are sensitive to rapid movement ... please use the Alternate code" — [ioccc.org 2006/sloane](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html); [ioccc.org 2000/thadgavin](https://www.ioccc.org/2000/thadgavin/index.html)

### Inferences
- The one-row scroll for sloane and eldby would show up as the top row dropping out or the image jittering. Three possible fixes: a 24-row pty if the renderer can clip, dropping the final newline (a one-character patch, which alters the original), or accepting it. This needs checking on the real wall.
- Output that is too tall (dhyang, hoyle) still works if the wall shows the last 23 lines, but the top of the picture is lost. For dhyang, the portrait of the source matters more than the run anyway.
- On the Reduced tier (about 15 rows), sloane and eldby would lose a third of the frame. thadgavin (which adapts to size), chaos (centred), sykes2 (7 rows) and westley clock3 (7 rows) would degrade most gracefully.

### Gaps
- I did not test on a real HUB75 wall or a Pi 4, or against the project's flash governor, so perceived flicker at 12-26 fps is unmeasured.
- I did not measure chaos's rotation frame rate or thadgavin's fps under `SLEEP` in detail.

## Which build cleanly on modern 64-bit gcc, or have fixes in the current IOCCC winner repo?

### Takeaway
Every shortlisted terminal entry builds on aarch64 with GCC 12 using the repo Makefiles, which keep `-Wall -Wextra` and add targeted `-Wno-…` flags rather than `-w`. On GCC 14, a plain `gcc -std=gnu17 -Wall` fails for almost all of them (implicit int and implicit declarations are errors). `-std=gnu89 -Wall` or `-fpermissive`, or simply the repo Makefile, fixes every shortlisted entry except herrmann2, which needs its Makefile. The two real GCC 14 Makefile failures in this era's interactive set are 2004/arachnid and 2006/toledo2.

### Cited Findings
- The repo's per-entry Makefile flags (sloane shown) are `CSTD= -std=gnu99`, `CWARN= -Wall -Wextra ${CSILENCE}`, where `CSILENCE` includes `-Wno-implicit-function-declaration -Wno-implicit-int -Wno-return-type -Wno-deprecated-non-prototype -Wno-main`, among others — [repo 2006/sloane/Makefile](https://github.com/ioccc-src/winner/blob/master/2006/sloane/Makefile)
- Results with the repo Makefile's default target, all **[local test]**:
  - **GCC 12.5 (all pass):** eldby, westley (1996), gandalf, chaos, dhyang, natori, herrmann2, ollinger, 2001/westley, newbern, hoyle, jdalbec, arachnid, kopczynski, 2005/sykes, sloane, sykes2, toledo1, toledo2, birken, gavare, primenum, night, aidan, 2005/toledo. thadgavin's `all` target fails only on its SDL half.
  - **GCC 14.4 (all pass except):**

### Inferences
- The safest build recipe for this era on the Pi is the repo Makefile, or `-std=gnu89 -Wall -O2 … -lm` (plus `-lncurses` for chaos and thadgavin). Either keeps `-Wall` without `-w`. Expect dozens of warnings for some entries (for example, sloane gives 28 warnings under GCC 12 plain `-Wall`).
- If the Pi runs Raspberry Pi OS Bookworm (GCC 12), plain `-std=gnu17 -Wall` would also work for most entries. A move to a GCC 14-era OS would break plain builds, so pin `-std=gnu89` or use the Makefiles either way.
- None of the shortlisted terminal entries needed source patches to build on 64-bit ARM. The only real 64-bit or ARM blocker found in this era was 2006/monge, which is x86-only.

### Gaps
- Not tested on a real Raspberry Pi 4 or on Raspberry Pi OS itself, only on aarch64 Debian-based `gcc` Docker images. glibc and ncurses versions may differ.
- I did not verify which GCC version the installation's Pi image uses.
- Runtime correctness was checked by eye in one short run per entry, not over the full 40-second window.

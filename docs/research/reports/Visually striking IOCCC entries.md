# The real IOCCC donut leads five portraits

Build the show around five entries: **2006/sloane "Homer's favorite" (the donut, by Andy Sloane), 1992/imc (Ian Collier's Mandelbrot), 2000/thadgavin (Gavin Buttimore and Thaddaeus Frogley's plasma), 2012/endoh1 (Yusuke Endoh's ASCII fluid) and 2020/endoh3 (Endoh's mirror-clock quine)**. Each one pairs a source file that works as a picture with a run that fills a monochrome 80x23 terminal and needs no keyboard. Together they cover 1992 to 2020 and credit five people. Every one was submitted before LLM coding tools existed in practice, which matters because the IOCCC's current rules explicitly allow them. The donut question has a clean answer. The famous donut-shaped `donut.c` was a blog post and **never won the IOCCC**. Sloane's actual winner, from the same year, is 2006/sloane: the same torus renderer spinning over an infinite checkerboard. Print and run that, with the plaque "Created by Andy Sloane, 2006, Not A.I." Build every entry with `-std=gnu89` or the repo's flags, add `-fsigned-char` everywhere (ARM Linux defaults to unsigned `char`), and use the IOCCC's own slowed-down "alt" versions. Also give the donut a 24-row pty under the status strip so its extra newline stops scrolling the frame. The biggest open risk is the wall size. At the Reduced tier's 15 rows the donut, the clock and several alternates clip, so the donut is a strong argument for the Full tier.

## Sloane's IOCCC winner is the donut to hang

Andy Sloane posted `donut.c` on his blog on **15 September 2006**, calling it "my first attempt at obfuscated C" and "pretty amateurish", and the post never mentions the IOCCC ([a1k0n.net](https://www.a1k0n.net/2006/09/15/obfuscated-c-donut.html)). Five days later he posted "Donut Mark II", a donut over a scrolling checkerboard, noting that "a version of this (the scroll text says 'IOCCC 2006' instead) was featured in the 2006 International Obfuscated C Code Contest" ([a1k0n.net](https://www.a1k0n.net/2006/09/20/obfuscated-c-donut-2.html)). That version is **2006/sloane, "Homer's favorite"**, his only IOCCC win ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html); [author record](https://github.com/ioccc-src/winner/blob/master/author/Andy_Sloane.json)). Sloane himself prefers it: "I'm most proud of my IOCCC 2006 winner" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=2745084)). The confusion is understandable. donut.c is the most famous obfuscated C program in the world, with front-page Hacker News runs of **237, 204, 196 and 135 points** plus YouTube coverage, while only two HN comments ever name `2006/sloane` ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=2787227); [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37548599); [a1k0n.net](https://www.a1k0n.net/2021/01/13/optimizing-donut.html)). The two programs share the torus math, the z-buffer and the cursor-home redraw, so the IOCCC winner delivers exactly the spinning shaded donut the user remembers, with the checkerboard and a scrolling "IOCCC 2006" logo on top.

**Recommendation: print and run 2006/sloane.** "Created by Andy Sloane, 2006, Not A.I." is accurate on every word, and a second line can read "IOCCC 2006 winner, 'Homer's favorite'". The year is safe. The 19th contest was labeled 2006 even though entries were accepted from 31 December 2006 to 28 February 2007 ([2006 rules](https://github.com/ioccc-src/winner/blob/master/2006/rules.txt)). Print `sloane.c`, the judged artwork: about 33 lines, 56 columns wide and 1,707 bytes, "shaped into a work of art, without being prefixed by any `#define` hacks, or even `#includes`" ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html)). Its silhouette is blocky, a checkerboard around a ring, not the round donut of donut.c. The author says "the shape of the source gives a hint about its output", and the pairing is honest. Check the shape by eye in a monospaced proof before printing. The licence is clear too. IOCCC content is CC BY-SA 4.0, and the FAQ asks that you "ask the IOCCC judges first", so put a small credit and licence line (or a QR code) on the plaque and send a courtesy email ([FAQ](https://www.ioccc.org/faq.html#ioccc_copyright); [license](https://www.ioccc.org/license.html)).

The wall should compile **`sloane.alt.c`**, the IOCCC's own variant, which differs from the original only by one inserted `usleep(S)` (default 75,000 µs, about 12 to 13 frames per second). The repo adds it "to not flash too quickly, which can be problematic for some people" and warns photosensitive viewers about the original ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/2006/sloane/index.html)). Throttling is mandatory. Unthrottled, the entry ran at about **1,750 frames per second** on an M1. Its full-screen, high-contrast `.`/`#` checkerboard, sweeping toward the viewer, is the largest flash risk in the set, so run it past the flash governor first and raise `S` if the governor dims it. Because the pipeline typewrites the source it compiles, the wall will show the alt file. That is a one-statement difference from the printed original and worth a line on the info sign.

**The 23-row newline issue is real.** Each frame is a cursor-home followed by 23 lines of 79 characters, each ending in a newline ([source](https://github.com/ioccc-src/winner/blob/master/2006/sloane/sloane.c)). On an exactly 23-row pty the final newline scrolls the screen up one line every frame. The pump reads at most 4 KB per tick (`show/terminal.py`) and a frame is about 1,860 bytes, so render ticks will often land mid-frame. The likely symptom is a torn image, with the lower part offset by a row. The cleanest fix needs no source change. Open this entry's pty at 24 rows and keep the strip. The renderer already draws only the first 23 pyte rows when the strip is shown (`show/renderer.py`), and sloane never writes anything to row 24; the cursor just lands there. The existing `full_screen = true` flag also fixes the scroll, but it turns the attribution into a 2-second flash every 10 seconds, which breaks the spec's rule that the author and "Not A.I." stay visible for the whole run. The same 24-row treatment suits donut.c and 1996/eldby.

A few other donut facts shape the build and the tier decision. On gcc 14 a bare compile of sloane.c **fails with 19 errors**. With `-std=gnu89 -Wall` it builds with 23 warnings (implicit `sin`/`cos`/`printf`, `-Wsequence-point`, `-Wparentheses`), which makes a satisfying BUILD screen. It renders correctly without `math.h` because gcc substitutes its built-in `sin` and `cos`. The **Reduced tier breaks it**: the checkerboard fills all 23 rows and the stride and center row are hard-coded, so 15 rows can only show a scrolled, clipped slice.

If the user's heart is set on the round donut-shaped print, there is one honest fallback: **print and run donut.c itself**, label it "donut.c, first published at a1k0n.net, 2006" and never call it an IOCCC winner. That path has three costs. donut.c carries **no licence**, so it needs Sloane's permission. It has no built-in delay, since the 2021 version with `usleep` is "a little misshapen" and dated 2021 ([a1k0n.net](https://www.a1k0n.net/2021/01/13/optimizing-donut.html)), so it depends on pty back-pressure for pacing. It also needs `-std=gnu89` (8 warnings) and fails outright under `-std=c23`. Avoid the hybrid of printing donut.c and running sloane. The wall typewrites the source it compiles, so the portrait and the screen would disagree in front of the audience.

## Four more picks pair a shaped page with a live wall

The other four were chosen by scoring the source as a print, the run as a wall show, and the practical fit: 80x23, one phosphor color, no input, about 40 seconds, and a `-Wall` build on 64-bit ARM. Era spread, author variety and the "Not A.I." claim were weighed on top of that. The strongest candidates share one trait: **the source's shape predicts the output**, so a visitor who studies the portrait and then presses the button sees the picture come alive.

**1992/imc, Ian Collier ("Best output").** The source is laid out as the Mandelbrot set's cardioid and bulb, and the judges joked that "the source (layout) is self-documenting!" Collier also arranged the macro names to spell "Ian Colliers Obfuscated C Code" ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/1992/imc/index.html)). In text mode it shades with a 16-level ramp, and `./imc -text -size 78 22 -mask 15 -limit 15` filled the screen with a clean monochrome Mandelbrot in an arm64 gcc 14 test (1 warning). The output is static and appears in under a second, so the run command should be a short script that walks through several zooms and Julia sets from the entry's `try.sh`, pausing a few seconds on each. It is the only pick that resizes cleanly to 15 rows (`-size 78 14`). It is the oldest pick and a perfect match of source to output.

**2000/thadgavin, Gavin Buttimore and Thaddaeus Frogley ("Most portable output").** The entry "prints a dazzeling moving image under multiple interfaces", and the judges said its curses mode "is quite good at that" ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/2000/thadgavin/index.html)). The 94-line source is shaped into swirling figures, one per back end (DOS, SDL and curses). The curses back end reads `COLS` and `LINES` and uses no color. In an 80x23 pty it filled the screen with a shaded plasma tunnel. It is the only full-screen animation among the candidates that adapts itself to the Reduced tier. Build only the curses alt target, since the SDL half fails without SDL1. Tune its `SLEEP` against the flash governor, because it redraws the whole screen.

**2012/endoh1, Yusuke Endoh ("Most complex ASCII fluid").** This is the community's favorite terminal piece after the donut. It has an 89-point HN story, a Hackaday feature, and 24 HN comment mentions ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20074185); [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2015/03/07/animated-ascii-fluid-dynamics-simulator-is-retro-cool/)). The source is block lettering that doubles as the fluid's starting layout, so `./endoh1 < endoh1.c` makes the printed portrait **collapse into liquid on the wall**, which may be the single best "code is art" moment available. Tests at 80x23 showed pure ASCII, lines of at most 79 columns, about 20 rows used, redraws in place at about 15 per second, and 0 warnings with the repo Makefile ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/2012/endoh1/index.html)). The author's own advice is a terminal "larger than 80 x 25", so confirm the fit on the real wall. Use the alt build, with `SLEEP` (default 12,321 µs) and `TIMER` set to 40 seconds (the maximum is 60). Avoid `endoh1_color.c`, which paints 256-color backgrounds.

**2020/endoh3, Yusuke Endoh ("Most head-turning").** The source is a 79x23 round clock face with numerals 1 to 12 in comments. The program prints its own source redrawn as a mirrored analog clock showing the compile time, and the official loop recompiles its output each cycle ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/2020/endoh3/index.html); [run_clock.sh](https://github.com/ioccc-src/winner/blob/master/2020/endoh3/run_clock.sh)). On the wall that means **the portrait itself ticks forward, with a real gcc compile between every tick**, the most literal version of the installation's premise. It builds with 0 warnings on gcc 12 and 14 under both gnu17 and gnu23, uses no color or input, and changes slowly enough to be flash-safe. It needs gcc and a writable directory at run time, both of which the sandbox allows. It does not fit 15 rows.

This set balances the two axes. imc and endoh3 lead with the print, thadgavin and the donut lead with the wall, and endoh1 is strong on both. The outputs are also distinct: a fractal gallery, an abstract plasma, a 3D object, a physics simulation and a self-rewriting clock. **Two Endoh entries out of five** is the one compromise. Endoh is the contest's most prolific visual author (in IOCCC29 alone he took three awards, part of the judges' "Hat trick of Hat-tricks"), and he owns the best 2018 to 2020 terminal pieces ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/2025/index.html)). To hold him to one, swap endoh3 for **1993/rince, James Bonfield**. Its source visibly contains a Space Invaders board built from cellular-automaton rules, and with no input the aliens march and drop bombs on their own in a 40x20 curses field (0 warnings, needs ncurses) ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/1993/rince/index.html)). The cost is that nothing in the set would be newer than 2012.

The ranked alternates each fix a different weakness. **2020/endoh2** (Star Wars opening crawl, about 30 seconds long, 5/5 output) is the most cinematic, but its 24-line frames plus a trailing newline scroll on 23 rows ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/2020/endoh2/index.html)). **1995/cdua, Carlos Duarte** has the best live output of its era: a maze carved and then solved on a buffer sized for exactly 80x23, finishing in about 25 seconds. Its source is a plain block, though, and it waits for Enter, so the run command must pipe in a few newlines ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/1995/cdua/index.html)). **2025/jingp49** (TARDIS-shaped source, a howlround that resolves into "DOCTOR WHO", takes its size as arguments and so fits 15 rows) scores 5/5 on both axes. It sits in the alternates for one reason, covered next.

### "Not A.I." favors the pre-2022 contests

The IOCCC now permits exactly what the plaques deny. The 2024 rules say entrants "are _allowed_ to use ... large language models (LLMs)" and only "ENCOURAGE" disclosure ([2024 rules](https://www.ioccc.org/2024/rules.html)). FAQ Q1.6 says the judges "do not discriminate on the basis of the tools used" ([FAQ](https://www.ioccc.org/faq.html#ai)), and the draft IOCCC30 rules repeat it ([next rules](https://www.ioccc.org/next/rules.html)). At least one celebrated 2025 winner took AI help. The GameBoy emulator 2025/ncw1 thanks "Gemini 3 Pro, for its ruthless code golfing suggestions" ([ioccc.org](https://www.ioccc.org/2025/ncw1/index.html)). Because disclosure is optional, a silent 2024 or 2025 README proves nothing, which is why all five recommendations come from 1992 to 2020 contests. jingp49's README contains no AI mention, but the only way to earn a "Not A.I." plaque for it is to ask the author and get a clear statement. If the author confirms, it becomes a strong fifth pick with its own story: people still out-write the machines in 2025. The 2020 set is the latest that is safe on timing alone.

## One flag set and one pty tweak keep every pick building

Every build test ran on aarch64 Linux in Docker on Apple Silicon, with gcc 12.5 (Bookworm-era), gcc 14.2 and 14.4 (Trixie-era). That matches the Pi 4's architecture and compiler generations but not its speed, so the results are strong evidence rather than proof. With the repo Makefiles, **110 of 111 winners from 1984 to 1995** built on both compilers ([winner repo](https://github.com/ioccc-src/winner)). Three facts drive the recipe. First, **gcc 14 turns implicit int and implicit declarations into hard errors**, so a bare `gcc -Wall` fails for almost every pre-2012 entry, while `-std=gnu89` (or `-fpermissive`) restores warnings-only behavior. Second, **`char` is unsigned on Linux/aarch64 but signed on x86 and on Apple-silicon macOS**. As a result, 2024/codemeow renders garbage, 2011/blakely segfaults, 2013/cable2 prints nothing and 1995/dodsond1 loops forever unless built with `-fsigned-char`, and a fix tested on a Mac can still hang on the Pi ([ioccc.org bugs](https://www.ioccc.org/bugs.html)). Third, the repo Makefiles pair `-Wall -Wextra` with long `-Wno-…` silencer lists, which satisfies "`-Wall`, never `-w`" but hides many of the warnings the BUILD screen is meant to show ([sloane Makefile](https://github.com/ioccc-src/winner/blob/master/2006/sloane/Makefile)). The pty runs its child with stdin and stderr on the terminal and `TERM=xterm` (`show/terminal.py`). Canned input therefore has to come from a shell redirect in the run command, and stderr chatter (such as imc's progress dots) will appear on the wall unless it is redirected.

### Global rules and open items

| Item | Rule or test | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Standard flag | Pin `-std=gnu89` for pre-2000 entries and sloane/thadgavin; `gnu17` for later ones; never rely on the compiler default | gcc 14 errors on implicit int; gcc 15 defaults to gnu23, which breaks donut.c and codemeow |
| `-fsigned-char` | Add to every entry | Matches the x86 platforms the code was written on; fixes four known ARM failures |
| Makefile defines | Copy each entry's `-D`/`-include` flags into `entry.toml` | endoh1, herrmann2, tmarrec and codemeow fail without them |
| Warnings on screen | Decide per entry: repo silencers (quiet) or bare `-std=gnu89 -Wall` (a screenful) | The spec says "real warnings are the point" |
| 24-row frames | Give sloane (and donut.c, eldby) a 24-row pty with the strip still drawn over row 24; confirm on the wall that no tearing remains | 23 newlines per frame scroll a 23-row screen |
| Color-dependent entries | Exclude eastman, birken, 2018/endoh2, 2024/weaver and 2025/endoh1 unless the renderer maps colors to brightness | The renderer draws glyphs only and ignores color; these carry their image in color (eastman, birken and weaver as background-colored spaces) |
| Non-ASCII glyphs | Exclude or map (`╱╲`, Braille) | Code points above 255 render as `?` |
| Curses entries | Test thadgavin (and rince) under `TERM=xterm` in pyte | Alternate-screen and cursor sequences are untested in pyte |
| gcc on the Pi | Run `gcc --version` on the actual Pi image | Bookworm (gcc 12) is more permissive than Trixie (gcc 14); neither is confirmed |
| Frame rates | Time endoh1, thadgavin and the endoh3 compile loop on a real Pi 4 | All measurements came from an M1; the Pi is much slower |
| Flash governor | Run sloane, thadgavin and endoh1 through it and record the dimming | Full-screen high-contrast change is exactly what it limits |
| 5x7 font legibility | Check the luminance ramps (`.,-~:;=!*#$@`, imc's `F7RQ&%#*`) at 15 to 40 feet | Letter "shades" may read as text rather than tone |
| Reduced tier | If chosen, only imc and thadgavin survive unchanged; the donut clips | Feed this into the week-2 tier decision |
| Prints | Proof every source in a monospaced font with tabs at 8 | Shapes break in proportional fonts; buzzard and tmarrec use tabs |
| Permissions | Email the IOCCC judges (courtesy under CC BY-SA 4.0); email Sloane only if using donut.c | FAQ Q10.1 asks first; donut.c has no licence |

## Conclusion

The research turns "which entries look best" into a sharper rule for this installation: pick programs whose source is a picture of what they draw. That rule favors the IOCCC's own donut over the internet's, puts imc's Mandelbrot and endoh1's self-melting source ahead of flashier color entries that would render as a blank wall, and gives endoh3 its place because the portrait literally ticks on screen. It also exposes the installation's two quiet dependencies. The "Not A.I." claim is only defensible for contests before 2024, so any modern entry needs the author's word, not a missing disclosure. And the wall's geometry matters as much as its brightness: one stray newline or a 15-row tier decides whether the most-loved piece in the show looks right.

The next useful step is a single evening on the real Pi and the four-panel prototype. Build all five with the global flag set, run each for 40 seconds through the flash governor, and settle three questions: whether the 24-row pty cures the donut's tear, whether thadgavin's and sloane's full-screen changes survive the governor at their default pacing, and which gcc the Pi image ships. Everything else in this report is already verified on matching architecture and compilers.

## Addendum (2026-09-29): the picks on the 128x64 proof of concept

The proof of concept is four 64x32 panels mounted 2 x 2, a 128x64 view. The full wall stays the end vision, and nothing above changes for it. At the 6x8 font cell, 128x64 holds only 21 columns by 8 rows, so the 80x23 terminal cannot fit. Decision Q53's default shows a 21x8 window of the wall and builds no scaled-down mode.

The sheets in `Visually striking IOCCC entries files/` (`poc-led.png`, `poc-distance.png`, made by `mock.py` from the researchers' captured runs) compare, for each pick, Q53's 21x8 window with an "ink" mode. In ink mode each terminal cell becomes one dot (1.6 px per column, keeping the font's 6:8 cell), lit by how much ink its glyph has or by the donut's own ramp `.,-~:;=!*#$@`.

- **The 21x8 window shows none of the five as a picture.** Each window is a patch of `#`, `@` and `=` glyphs, and imc and endoh3 show a few stray characters. It still suits the build step (source and compiler warnings as text).
- **The ink mode turns ASCII art back into the image it stands for.**
  - **thadgavin's plasma** is a smooth gradient and reads best of the five.
  - **The original donut.c** is a clean shaded torus on black.
  - **endoh3's clock face and hand** are recognizable.
  - **endoh1's fluid** body is visible.
- **2006/sloane is busy at this size.** In ink mode its checkerboard is as bright as the donut. With the donut ramp the donut stands out, but the frame is still cluttered. For the proof of concept alone, donut.c looks far better, with the provenance and licence caveats above.
- **imc** is hard to read. The capture used was not the recommended `-text -size 78 22` run, so re-capture it before judging.

Open for the owner: whether the proof of concept should get an ink mode (a renderer option, not built; Q53 says no scaled-down mode), or should show the source and build in the window and accept that the output is not visible until the full wall.
